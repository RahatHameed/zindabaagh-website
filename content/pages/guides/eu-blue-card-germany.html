The EU Blue Card is Germany's residence permit for university graduates and experienced IT specialists who have a job offer. It is the route most skilled workers aim for, because it leads to permanent residence faster than any other work permit.

Who can get it

You need three things:

  • A qualification. A German university degree, or a foreign degree that is recognised or comparable. IT specialists can qualify without a degree if they have at least three years of comparable professional experience within the last seven years.
  • A job offer. A contract or binding offer for at least six months, in a job that fits your qualification.
  • A minimum salary. The threshold is set new every January.

Shortage occupations include IT, engineering, natural sciences, medicine, nursing, teaching and some management roles in production, construction and logistics. Only the fixed gross salary counts. A bonus that is not guaranteed in the contract usually does not.

Documents to prepare

  • Passport
  • Signed employment contract or binding job offer
  • The "declaration of employment" form, filled in by your employer
  • Degree certificate, plus a printout from the anabin database showing your university and degree are recognised. If they are not listed, you need a Statement of Comparability from the ZAB.
  • For regulated professions such as doctors: the licence to practise
  • CV, biometric photos, application form and the visa fee
  • Proof of health insurance from your first day in Germany

The German mission in your country publishes its own checklist. Follow that one exactly.

How to apply

  1. Check your degree in anabin and your salary against the threshold.
  2. Apply for a national visa at the German embassy or consulate where you live. Many countries can apply online through the Consular Services Portal.
  3. Enter Germany with the visa, register your address, and apply for the Blue Card itself at your local immigration office (Ausländerbehörde) before the visa expires.

If your employer wants to speed things up, they can start the fast-track procedure for skilled workers at the immigration office in Germany, for a fee.

What the Blue Card gives you

  • Permanent residence after {{facts.settlement.blueCardA1Months}} months, or after {{facts.settlement.blueCardB1Months}} months if you have German at B1.
  • Family: your spouse can join you without proving German skills, and may work without restriction.
  • Changing jobs: possible. In the first twelve months you must tell the immigration office, which can check the new job.

If your salary is too low

You are not out of options. With a recognised degree or recognised vocational training you can apply for the skilled-worker residence permit, which has no fixed salary threshold for most applicants under 45. The wait for permanent residence is longer, {{facts.settlement.skilledWorkerMonths}} months.
