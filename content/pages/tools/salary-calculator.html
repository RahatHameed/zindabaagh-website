What comes off a German salary

  • Income tax (Lohnsteuer): progressive. The first {{facts.tax.basicAllowance|eur}} of taxable income is tax-free in {{facts.year}}, and the rate rises from 14% to 42%.
  • Pension insurance: {{facts.social.pensionRate}}% of gross, up to {{facts.social.ceilingPension|eur}} a year.
  • Unemployment insurance: {{facts.social.unemploymentRate}}%, same ceiling.
  • Health insurance: {{facts.social.healthRate}}% plus half of your insurer's additional contribution, up to {{facts.social.ceilingHealth|eur}} a year.
  • Long-term care insurance: {{facts.social.careRate}}%, plus {{facts.social.careChildlessExtra}}% if you are over 23 and have no children.
  • Solidarity surcharge: only on higher incomes. Church tax: 9% of income tax (8% in Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg) for members.

Your employer pays roughly the same social contributions again on top of your gross salary.

How exact is this?

It is an estimate for an employee with public health insurance. It leaves out things that change the result by a little: reduced care contributions for several children, the different care split in Saxony, private health insurance, company cars and other benefits. For an exact figure use the Finance Ministry's official wage tax calculator.
