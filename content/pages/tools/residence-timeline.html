Settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis)

The settlement permit is permanent. It is not tied to an employer and does not expire. How long you wait depends on your current permit:

  • EU Blue Card: {{facts.settlement.blueCardA1Months}} months, or {{facts.settlement.blueCardB1Months}} months with German at B1. You need A1 at least.
  • Skilled worker: {{facts.settlement.skilledWorkerMonths}} months of holding the permit and paying pension contributions, with German at B1.
  • After a German degree or German vocational training: {{facts.settlement.germanGraduateMonths}} months of working as a skilled worker, with German at B1.
  • Everyone else: {{facts.settlement.generalMonths}} months, {{facts.settlement.generalMonths}} months of pension contributions and German at B1.

In every case you also need a secure income, enough living space, and pension contributions for the whole waiting period.

Citizenship (Einbürgerung)

You can apply after {{facts.citizenshipYears}} years of legal residence in Germany. You need German at B1, a passed citizenship test, and an income that covers you and your family without social benefits. Germany allows you to keep your other nationality. The faster three-year track was abolished in 2025.

Source: Make it in Germany, settlement permit. Your local naturalisation office (Einbürgerungsbehörde) handles citizenship applications.
